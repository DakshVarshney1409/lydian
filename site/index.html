What it checks

The things a regulator would ask about.

Written for insurance first. The same checks transfer directly to lending, benefits, and any domain where the answer has to match a document that changes. Five are exercised by the benchmark today; coverage determination and required disclosure are on the roadmap.

CheckWhat it catchesComponent
Policy currency A superseded schedule or an expired exclusion quoted as if in force on the date of loss. retriever
Coverage determination Covered called excluded, or the reverse, against the governing document. reasoner
Monetary accuracy Deductibles, sub-limits and caps quoted from the wrong plan year. retriever
Condition completeness The cap, the reporting deadline, the endorsement prerequisite — dropped, so conditional coverage reads as unconditional. reasoner
Question fidelity A pre-filing coverage question answered with filing instructions. Half a two-part question answered. intent
Confidence calibration Thin or absent evidence stated flatly, with no signal to the customer that it was a guess. responder
Required disclosure Appeal rights, endorsement requirements or adjuster referral missing from a reply that needed them. responder

Each row is a failing test case with a named component, not a score between one and five. The component is what makes it a ticket instead of a discussion.

How we're different

Other tools give you a score. We tell you which node to fix.

Tracing and eval platforms are good at recording what your agent did and grading what came out. They stop at "this answer scored 2/5". Lydian starts there. Node-level root cause works today; test-set drafting, regression runs and trace adapters are on the roadmap.

You already have AI engineers

They can debug it. That was never the problem.

Lydian isn't a substitute for that skill. It exists because of what the skill costs, and what it never gets pointed at.

Cost

Diagnosis competes with shipping

Every incident quietly converts a build day into an investigation day. Nobody puts that on the roadmap.

Coverage

You only debug what you hear about

The confidently wrong answer nobody escalated stays wrong. A suite runs on all of it, every time.

Regression

Nobody re-checks the forty cases that worked

A prompt edit to fix one complaint is the most common way a working agent breaks.

Evidence

It ends the retrieval-vs-prompt argument

Two engineers disagreeing is expensive and unresolvable by opinion. A verdict with the innocent nodes cleared settles it.

An attention and evidence gap, not a skill gap. Strong teams have it too — they just fix the node in an hour once they know which one, instead of spending the hour finding out.
